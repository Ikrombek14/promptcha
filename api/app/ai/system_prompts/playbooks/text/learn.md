# Learn: explanation, tutoring, summary
## Required facts
- topic: the exact thing to explain or learn (one concept, one skill, one text to summarise) | ask: What exactly do you want to understand or learn? | default: the user's topic narrowed to one concept or one question
- level: what the learner already knows (complete beginner, school student, university, professional switching fields) | ask: What is your current level with this topic? | default: complete beginner, no prior knowledge
- purpose: why they need it (exam, work task, curiosity, teaching someone else, a daily practice routine) | ask: What do you need it for — an exam, work, or just to understand? | default: to understand and be able to explain it to someone else
- format: how they want to learn (one explanation, a step-by-step lesson, a daily practice with Q&A, a summary of a text, flashcards) | ask: How should it be delivered — one explanation, a lesson series, or practice with questions? | default: one explanation with examples and 3 check questions
- language: language of the answer, and whether key terms should be kept in English | ask: Which language, and should technical terms stay in English? | default: Uzbek (Latin) with key terms in English in brackets
- time: how much time the learner has per session or in total | ask: How much time do you have (per day or in total)? | default: 15 minutes per session
## Must include
- The learner's level and purpose in the Context section so the depth is fixed
- Explanation rule: "explain as if to a curious 12-year-old, then add one level of depth for the stated level"
- At least one real, local or everyday example per key point (bazaar, taxi, Telegram, school, a small shop) — examples are mandatory, not optional
- Structure with counts: 3–5 key points, one analogy, one common misconception corrected, 3 self-check questions with answers hidden or given at the end
- Length as a number (explanation 200–400 words; a lesson under 600 words; summary 10% of the source or max 150 words)
- Honesty rule: "if you are not sure or the information may be outdated, say so instead of guessing"
- Answer language, script and the term-handling rule (English terms in brackets)
## Quality rules
- Define every new term in one plain sentence the first time it appears; no undefined jargon
- Progression: simple picture first, then the precise definition, then the example, then the exception — in that order
- For tutoring sessions: one step at a time, wait for the learner's reply, correct max 3 mistakes per turn with a one-line reason, praise briefly and specifically
- For summaries: keep the author's claims separate from the model's commentary; include 3 bullets "what to remember" and 1 bullet "what is unclear"
- For exam preparation: match the exam format (DTM test, IELTS task type, school oral exam) and include 2 practice items in that exact format
- Ban vague encouragement ("it's easy, don't worry") — replace with a concrete next step
- Require a final "next step" line: what to study or practise tomorrow, in one sentence
- If the topic is broad, the model must propose 3 sub-topics and ask which one to start with rather than covering everything shallowly
## Avoid
- Encyclopedia-style dumps with no examples, no questions and no level adjustment
- Assuming prior knowledge the user did not claim (formulas, English terms without translation)
- Guessing facts, dates or numbers instead of flagging uncertainty
- Bundling several subjects or a whole semester into one prompt
- Answering in a mix of languages when one was requested
