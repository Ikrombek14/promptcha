# Brand identity brief
## Required facts
- brand_name: the exact name and how it is written (Latin/Cyrillic, capitalisation) | ask: What is the brand name, exactly as it should be written? | default: [brend nomi]; never a generic "BRAND"
- business: what the brand sells, to whom, in which city or channel | ask: What does the brand do, and who are the customers? | default: from the user's text; ask if missing
- personality: 3 adjectives for the brand's character (warm, modern, trustworthy; playful, bold, youthful) | ask: Describe the brand in 3 words? | default: friendly, modern, trustworthy
- audience: the main customer with age, situation and language | ask: Who is the main customer? | default: people in Uzbekistan aged 20–45, Uzbek/Russian speakers, mobile-first
- competitors: 1–3 local competitors and how the brand should differ | ask: Who are your competitors, and how should you look different? | default: [raqobatchilar] placeholder; the brief asks for a one-line difference
- deliverables: what is needed now (logo, colors, fonts, Instagram templates, business card, sign, packaging) | ask: What do you need first — logo, colors, social templates, business card? | default: logo + color palette + typography + Instagram avatar and post template
## Must include
- Brand summary: name (in quotes), what it does, for whom, the one-line difference from competitors, 3 personality words
- Deliverables list with sizes: logo (primary, icon, one-color), palette (primary, secondary, neutral, hex placeholders), typography (1 heading font, 1 body font, both supporting Uzbek Latin and Cyrillic characters), Instagram avatar 1:1, post template 4:5, story 9:16, business card 90×50 mm, sign
- Color rules: max 3 brand colors + neutrals, contrast ratio at least 4.5:1 for text on background, one accent for calls to action
- Logo rules: works in one color, readable at 32 px and on a shop sign, clear space equal to the mark's height
- Tone of voice: 3 do / 3 don't examples in the audience's language (e.g. "siz" address, no corporate phrases)
- Usage examples to produce: a Telegram post, an Instagram post, a storefront mockup — described, not invented facts
- Output format: a structured brief with headings and a checklist so the user can hand it to a designer or an AI tool
## Quality rules
- Everything measurable: color count, font count, sizes in mm/px, contrast ratio, number of logo variants (3: full, icon, one-color)
- Fonts must support Uzbek Latin characters (ʻ, oʻ, gʻ, sh, ch) and Cyrillic — state it as a hard requirement
- No brand names as references ("like Apple") — describe qualities instead (minimal, geometric, warm)
- Local fit: where the brand lives (Telegram, Instagram, bazaar sign, Uzum listing) determines the first deliverables; print sizes in mm, screen sizes in px
- Cultural elements, if used, named precisely (suzani motif, ikat stripe, tandoor silhouette) and limited to one motif
- Include a "what not to do" list: gradients on the logo, more than 3 colors, decorative fonts for body text, low-contrast pastel text
- Ask for 2 directions (e.g. warm-traditional vs clean-modern) with one line each, then the user chooses — not 5 directions
- Length: under 400 words; a brief, not a strategy book
## Avoid
- A 15-section "brand strategy" with mission, vision and values essays for a small shop
- Inventing the brand's history, awards, taglines or customer numbers
- Choosing colors or fonts without stating why (mood, readability, contrast)
- Copying a known brand's identity
- Requesting a full website and campaign in the same brief
