# Story: narrative, cinematic scene
## Required facts
- scene: the single most important moment of the story (who, where, what happens) — the tool makes one shot | ask: What is the key moment to show — who, where, what happens? | default: the emotional peak of the user's story as one continuous action
- character: the main character in 3–5 fixed traits (age, clothing, one accessory, hair) for consistency | ask: Describe the main character in a few traits (age, clothing, hair)? | default: one character with 4 fixed traits stated by the model and reused across clips
- mood: genre and feeling (warm family drama, adventure, mystery, documentary, fairy tale) | ask: What genre or mood? | default: warm, calm, cinematic realism
- setting: place and time (a village in Fergana valley at dusk, 1980s Tashkent apartment, a fantasy bazaar) | ask: Where and when does it happen? | default: a real Uzbek location named precisely, golden hour
- format: aspect ratio and where it will be shown (16:9 YouTube/screen, 9:16 social) | ask: Horizontal for YouTube or vertical for social? | default: horizontal 16:9
- clips: how many clips the story needs (1 or a sequence of 3–5) | ask: One clip or a sequence of several? | default: one clip; if the story clearly needs more, 3 clips with a shared look line
## Must include
- Shot type and one camera move first ("wide establishing shot, slow push-in", "over-the-shoulder tracking")
- Character with the fixed traits, doing one continuous action with a clear emotion visible in body language
- Setting, time of day, weather and light source ("low sun from the right", "single oil lamp")
- Look and grade as film language ("shot on 35mm, soft halation, muted teal-amber grade", "documentary handheld")
- Duration and pacing ("8 seconds, slow, contemplative"), format at the end
- Sound only for tools that support it: ambient sound and at most one short line of dialogue in quotes with the language named ("in Uzbek")
- Continuity line for sequences: the same look, palette, character traits and lens repeated verbatim in every clip prompt
## Quality rules
- One clip = one beat of the story; a sequence is 3–5 clip prompts (establish → action → reaction/resolution), each 60–120 words, numbered
- Show emotion through action and light, not adjectives ("she pauses, hand on the door frame" not "she feels sad")
- Physically plausible motion, slow-to-medium; no fights, no fast running crowds, no readable signs
- Character consistency: the trait list appears identically in each clip; for Seedance/Kling image-to-video, note "use the reference image for the character"
- Local authenticity: architecture, clothing and objects named correctly (mahalla courtyard, chapan, suzani, tapchan, tandoor); no orientalist clichés
- No celebrity likeness, no copyrighted characters or film titles as references; genre references are eras and techniques ("1970s Soviet cinema look")
- Text-free frames; titles and subtitles are added in editing
- The prompt ends with duration and format; nothing after
## Avoid
- A whole plot in one prompt with "then… later… finally…"
- Vague characters ("a man") that change appearance from clip to clip
- Dialogue-heavy scenes for tools without audio, or several speaking lines in one clip
- Fantasy elements mixed with a realistic documentary look without stating one dominant style
- Asking for a specific famous film's style
